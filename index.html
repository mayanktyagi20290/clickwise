    How the numbers work

    Google's own data

    Totals match Search Console exactly for the same dates. Fresh, incomplete days are left out.

    Ranges, not promises

    Click gains are low–high estimates from your site's own click-through by position, and labelled as estimates.

    Measured vs inferred

    AI visibility built on signals, not observed answers, is marked inferred everywhere it appears.

    Rule-based insights

    Explanations come from stated rules, not a model guessing. Facts and likely causes are kept apart.

    Questions

    What access does Clickwise need?

    Read-only access to Search Console (the webmasters.readonly scope). Clickwise can't change anything in your account, and you can revoke access any time from your Google account settings.

    Where is my data stored?

    Search data is never stored: it is fetched from Google straight into the page and analysed there. Your settings, tasks and priority weights are kept in your browser for each site, and Settings › Forget this site deletes them.

    How are the click estimates worked out?

    For each search, Clickwise compares its click-through rate with what your own site usually earns at that position (an industry benchmark where you don't have enough data), and shows the gap as a low–high range of clicks per month. Each search is counted once, outliers are capped, and searches that look answered on the results page are flagged. Use the numbers to prioritise, not as a forecast.

    How does it tell whether a change worked?

    It compares equal windows before and after the date the change went live, in daily clicks, and checks the change is clear of day-to-day noise and larger than the whole site's movement over the same days. Other edits in the same windows and seasonal swings are listed as possible causes.

    Can I use it without signing in with Google?

    Yes. Export the Performance report from Search Console as Excel and upload it. A few details need sign-in, such as which search lands on which page and before/after for a single page.

    How is this different from Ahrefs or Semrush?

    Those tools estimate your rankings from their own crawls. Clickwise uses Google's first-party data for your site — real impressions, clicks and positions — and focuses on what to fix next and whether it worked.
